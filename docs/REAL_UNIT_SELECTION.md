# Choosing a real unit from the public record (selection memo)

**Status, 2026-10-29:** Bayer CropScience selected by the project owner. Built: a sourced process model and a generated drawing, both checked against Figure 10; a checked digitization of Figure 11, replayed through the model's interlocks; and an overview screen with the report's timeline walked through beside it ([BAYER_RT.md](BAYER_RT.md)). The candidates and page
references were researched from the CSB reports themselves and spot-checked against the PDFs' text.

## Why a real unit

Everything so far has one of two gaps. The OPEN100 sheets are real drawings with no process data. The Tennessee
Eastman twin has process data from a public simulator, but its drawing is generated by this project, so its scores
are a round trip ([entry 44](JOURNAL.md#44-one-plant-end-to-end-draw-the-te-model-in-the-house-style-and-read-it-back)).
A US Chemical Safety Board (CSB) investigation report can close both at once for one unit: it describes the process,
publishes a figure of it drawn by someone else, and sometimes publishes the control-system trends from the day.

The plan is the TE path from entries 44, 50 and 51, run on a real unit:

1. Write the process model from the report, every item citing its page or figure.
2. Read the control loops, interlocks and alarm setpoints from the report's narrative into the model.
3. Generate the drawing in the house style. The CSB figure is then an **independent check** of the generated
   drawing, which the TE round trip never had.
4. Digitise the published trends, state the digitising error, and replay them through the existing replay server,
   operator screens and normal bands.
5. The incident is the hazard citation, instead of a keyword match from the public incident corpus.

## Recommendation: Bayer CropScience residue treater

It is the only candidate with several variables trended over hours, and its report spells out the control and
interlock logic, so the twin can show the published trends on screens built from a model of that logic. One vessel is
a fair scope for a generated drawing checked against the report's own figure.

**Biggest risk:** the report gives no instrument tag numbers for the residue treater. Every tag on our drawing would be
assigned by this project, as the TE tags are, and labelled that way; the CSB figure can check the drawing at the level
it is drawn (instrument types, valves, connections), not tags. The trends are low-resolution images: digitising Fig 11
gives roughly ±1 °C, ±0.5 psig and ±2 minutes, and those bounds travel with every replayed value.

## Reuse and licence: to decide before building

- csb.gov pages carry the footer "© U.S. Chemical Safety Board. All Rights Reserved." The site does not state a reuse
  policy that we found. US federal government works are generally not protected by copyright in the US (17 U.S.C.
  §105), but the footer says otherwise, and some figures reproduce company material (DCS screens, hazard-analysis
  excerpts) without a credit line. This is not legal advice.
- The safe default: **do not republish CSB figures or screen captures.** Link and cite them by page and figure. The
  model, the generated drawing and the screens are this project's own work, written from facts the report states. The
  digitised values are facts, cited to the figure they come from.

## Presentation

People died in each of these events. The twin shows what the published data showed and when. It does not claim the
screens would have prevented anything, does not second-guess the operators, and every page links the CSB report.

## What would make this succeed

- The generated drawing matches the CSB figure on every instrument type, valve and connection the figure shows,
  scored by the same audit approach as entry 50, with differences listed rather than tuned away.
- Every replayed value traces to a figure and a digitising error bound.
- The interlocks and setpoints in the model each cite a report page.
